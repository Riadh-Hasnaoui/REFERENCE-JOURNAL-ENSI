\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

This section compares our review with the most closely related prior surveys, highlighting
the specific contribution of our repository representation focus.

\subsection{Broad Surveys of LLM-Based Software Engineering}

Hou et al.~\cite{hou2024llmse} present a systematic literature review of LLMs for software
engineering that covers over 200 primary studies across tasks including code generation,
summarization, testing, and repair. This is the most comprehensive general survey in the
area. However, it does not focus on repository representation: its primary dimensions of
analysis are the SE task, the model architecture, and the evaluation methodology, rather than
the representational strategy. Our survey is complementary: where Hou et al.\ ask ``what
tasks can LLMs perform?'' we ask ``how do LLMs represent the repository to perform those tasks?''

Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026survey} survey LLMs specifically for software engineering with
emphasis on the evolution from specialized code models to general-purpose LLMs. Like Hou et
al., this survey is organized around tasks and models rather than representation paradigms.
Jiang et al.~\cite{jiang2026survey} survey LLMs for code generation, providing extensive
coverage of generation techniques but limited discussion of repository-level context management.

\subsection{Task-Specific Surveys}

Husein et al.~\cite{husein2025codecompletion} survey LLMs for code completion, covering models
and benchmarks in detail. While this survey overlaps with our T1 coverage, it is limited to
the completion task and does not address the representation problem across the full range of SE
tasks. Moreover, it does not adopt a paradigm-centric perspective: studies are organized
chronologically by model rather than by representational strategy.

Tao et al.~\cite{tao2026ragsurvey} survey retrieval-augmented code generation with a focus
on repository-level approaches. This is the survey most closely related to our work, as it
specifically addresses the role of retrieval in repository-level code tasks. However, it treats
RAG as the \emph{only} paradigm and does not compare it against graph-based, summarization-based,
dynamic, or agentic alternatives. Our survey subsumes this work as a deep examination of one
of our seven paradigms (\parcmd{2}).

\subsection{Benchmark and Evaluation Surveys}

Several papers survey benchmarks for repository-level SE rather than techniques. These include
Du et al.~\cite{du2025dependeval} on dependency understanding benchmarks, Vergopoulos et
al.~\cite{vergopoulos2025automated} on automated benchmark generation, and
Pavel et al.~\cite{pavel2025swemera} on dynamic benchmark construction. These survey the
evaluation landscape rather than the representation landscape and are complementary to our work.

Our survey is unique in adopting a paradigm-centric, repository-representation-focused
perspective that spans all seven major SE tasks and all seven identified paradigms. The
five-dimensional trade-off framework and the bidirectional task--paradigm mapping are original
contributions not present in any prior survey. The 160-study corpus represents the most
comprehensive collection of repository-level LLM-based SE studies assembled to date.
