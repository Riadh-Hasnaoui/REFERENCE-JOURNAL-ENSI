\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

We organise the related literature into three categories: (1) broad surveys on
LLMs for Software Engineering, (2) focused surveys on specific SE sub-tasks and
LLM techniques, and (3) primary studies that constitute the corpus of this SLR
and are discussed in detail in Sections~\ref{sec:taxonomy}
and~\ref{sec:semapping}.

\subsection{Broad Surveys on LLMs for Software Engineering}
\label{subsec:broadsurveys}

\paragraph{Hou et al.\ (2024) — ACM TOSEM~\cite{hou2024llmse_slr}.}
The most comprehensive SLR on LLMs for SE to date.
The authors analyse 395 papers from January 2017 to January 2024 and organise
their findings around four research questions: LLM types, data collection
methods, SE tasks, and evaluation strategies.
The survey is invaluable as a \emph{census} of the field.
Its limitation, for the purposes of the present work, is precisely its breadth:
with dozens of SE tasks competing for attention, the mechanisms by which LLMs
receive repository context are addressed only tangentially.
No dedicated taxonomy of repository representation strategies is proposed,
and the cross-file dependency challenge — which our inclusion criterion IC2
places at the centre of the RRP — is not analysed as a first-class concern.
Moreover, the temporal cutoff of January 2024 predates the agentic wave
(PAR-7) and the emergence of memory-augmented approaches (PAR-5) as mature
research directions.

\paragraph{Zhang et al.\ (2026) — Science China Information
Sciences~\cite{zhang2026surveyse}.}
A recent and highly cited survey covering LLMs for SE published in a
Chinese-English bilingual journal.
The authors organise their review around LLM architectures (encoder-only,
decoder-only, encoder-decoder) and SE tasks, providing a useful update that
extends the Hou et al.\ temporal coverage to mid-2025.
The perspective is \emph{model-centric} rather than \emph{task-centric} at the
representation level: the survey asks \emph{which model} is used for each SE
task rather than \emph{how} that model ingests a repository.
Like Hou et al., the context construction pipeline — the input side of every
LLM-based SE system — is treated as an implementation detail rather than a
primary research dimension.
Our taxonomy fills this gap systematically.

\subsection{Task-Focused and Paradigm-Focused Surveys}
\label{subsec:focusedsurveys}

\paragraph{Husein et al.\ (2025) — Computer Standards \&
Interfaces~\cite{husein2025llmcompletion_slr}.}
An SLR specifically on LLM-based code completion, making it the most directly
adjacent prior work to the code-completion thread of our review.
The authors study deep learning and transformer-based approaches and provide a
thorough treatment of completion at the function and file level.
Two gaps are salient.
First, the inclusion criteria focus on function-level and single-file completion;
cross-file and repository-level techniques appear only at the periphery.
Second, the survey precedes the wide adoption of RAG architectures and
agentic frameworks in code completion research (2024--2026), which constitute
PAR-2 and PAR-7 respectively — the two most populous paradigms in our corpus
(35 and 41 primary studies, respectively).
Our SLR explicitly targets the repository-level setting and covers all seven
representation paradigms with temporal coverage extending to early 2026.

\paragraph{Jiang et al.\ (2026) — ACM TOSEM~\cite{jiang2026surveycodegen}.}
A thorough survey of LLMs for code generation published in the same venue as
the present work.
Jiang et al.\ taxonomise generation tasks (text-to-code, code-to-code,
commit message generation, test generation) and evaluate models across
benchmarks.
The survey's contribution is substantial; its scope is, however, orthogonal to
ours.
Where Jiang et al.\ focus on the \emph{output} side of the generation pipeline
(what is generated and how it is evaluated), we focus on the
\emph{input} side (how a repository is represented so that generation can
proceed).
A practitioner using the two surveys together obtains a complete picture: our
work explains how to feed the model; Jiang et al.\ explain what the model
produces.

\paragraph{Tao et al.\ (2026) — arXiv~\cite{tao2026ragsurvey}.}
The survey most thematically proximate to our work: a dedicated review of
Retrieval-Augmented Code Generation (RACG) with explicit focus on
repository-level approaches.
Tao et al.\ provide the most thorough existing treatment of what we term PAR-2
(Retrieval-Augmented Context Construction), including indexing strategies,
retrieval metrics, and re-ranking techniques.
Our review builds on and significantly extends this work in four directions.
\emph{First}, we identify six additional representation paradigms (PAR-1,
PAR-3 through PAR-7) that lie outside the RAG boundary and are not covered by
Tao et al.
\emph{Second}, we analyse each paradigm across 10 structural dimensions (see
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), providing a comparative analytical framework that
PAR-2-only surveys cannot offer.
\emph{Third}, we map all seven paradigms against seven SE objectives, revealing
that PAR-2 dominates code completion but is substantially less used for program
repair (where PAR-4 and PAR-7 prevail) and for code search (where PAR-1 and
PAR-3 dominate).
\emph{Fourth}, our 5-pillar protocol yields a fully PRISMA-documented inclusion
process, enabling future replication and updates.
Tao et al.\ do not report a PRISMA flow diagram or checklist.

\subsection{Methodological Positioning}
\label{subsec:methpositioning}

\paragraph{Added value of the 5-pillar methodology.}
None of the five surveys reviewed above follows all five methodological pillars
that we employ:
Kitchenham's SLR guidelines~\cite{kitchenham2007guidelines},
Wohlin's snowballing procedure~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing},
PRISMA 2020 reporting standards~\cite{page2021prisma},
Cruzes and Dyb{\aa}'s thematic synthesis~\cite{cruzes2011recommended},
and the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards~\cite{acmsigsoft2020standards}.

detail.

The integration of Wohlin's snowballing in our protocol is particularly
important: it allows us to capture high-impact works that are indexed in venues
not covered by the four primary bibliographic databases (IEEE Xplore, ACM DL,
Springer Link, arXiv), specifically workshop papers, preprints, and
non-standardly indexed conference proceedings.
The Cruzes and Dyb{\aa} thematic synthesis method provides an explicit
procedure for moving from raw data extraction to higher-level conceptual
categories, reducing analyst bias in the taxonomy construction.
The ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards provide a reporting checklist that ensures
no methodological component is omitted from the final manuscript.

The combination of these five pillars results in a review that is not only more
comprehensive in scope, but also more \emph{reproducible}: every decision in
the search-to-synthesis pipeline is documented in the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram
(Figure~\ref{fig:prisma}) and the accompanying PRISMA checklist (Online
Supplement~A).

\subsection{Summary: The Unique Contribution of This SLR}
\label{subsec:uniquecontribution}

The fundamental contribution of this paper is not merely \emph{more} coverage
of a well-studied topic.
It is a \emph{different lens}: the Repository Representation Problem.

None of the five surveys above — individually or collectively — provides:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A formal definition of the RRP as a constrained optimisation problem
        (Section~\ref{subsec:rrp});
  \item A seven-paradigm taxonomy grounded in 159 primary studies, with each
        paradigm covering a structurally distinct approach to context
        construction;
  \item A 10-dimension analytical framework enabling systematic
        paradigm-to-paradigm comparison;
  \item A cross-axis mapping of paradigms against seven SE objectives,
        identifying paradigm–task affinity and paradigm–task gaps;
  \item Empirical evidence of the static-repository evaluation bias
        (10--61\%) and its differential impact on the seven paradigms;
  \item A five-pillar SLR methodology with full PRISMA 2020 documentation
        enabling future replication.
\end{enumerate}

The five surveys above provide the \emph{landscape}; this SLR provides the
\emph{map}.
For a practitioner designing a repository-level LLM system, or a researcher
seeking to position a new contribution within the field, the taxonomy and the
cross-axis mapping in this paper are the primary tools to consult.
