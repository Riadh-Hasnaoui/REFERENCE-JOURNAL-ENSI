\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented the first systematic literature review dedicated to the
\emph{Repository Representation Problem} (RRP) — the challenge of selecting,
encoding, and injecting the right subset of a large codebase into a finite LLM
context window.
Analysing 159 primary studies published between 2020 and early 2026, and
following a five-pillar SLR methodology (Kitchenham~\cite{kitchenham2007guidelines},
Wohlin~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing}, PRISMA~2020~\cite{page2021prisma},
Cruzes and Dyb{\aa}~\cite{cruzes2011recommended},
ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards~\cite{acmsigsoft2020standards}), we produced:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{seven-paradigm taxonomy} (PAR-1 through PAR-7) grounded in
        the corpus, with formal definitions and boundaries;
  \item A \textbf{10-dimension analytical framework} enabling systematic
        comparison across paradigms;
  \item A \textbf{paradigm--SE task mapping matrix} across seven SE objectives;
  \item Empirical evidence of a \textbf{10--61\% static-repository evaluation
        bias} affecting three paradigms~\cite{zheng2025humanevo};
  \item A structured \textbf{11-challenge research agenda}.
\end{enumerate}

The taxonomy reveals a field in rapid transition.
Between 2024 and 2026, agentic systems (PAR-7) overtook retrieval-augmented
systems (PAR-2) as the dominant paradigm by study count and benchmark
performance~\cite{yang2024sweagent,zhang2025swebenchlive}.
Memory-augmented approaches (PAR-5) emerged as a new research thread
\cite{wang2026codemem,pan2026prometheus}, and hybrid paradigm combinations
(PAR-2 + PAR-3~\cite{liu2025codexgraph}, PAR-5 + PAR-7
\cite{ma2025lingmaagent}) are becoming common.

The RRP lens offered by this paper is complementary to — not in competition
with — task-oriented surveys such as Hou et al.~\cite{hou2024llmse_slr},
Jiang et al.~\cite{jiang2026surveycodegen}, and Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026surveyse}.
Those surveys tell researchers \emph{what} LLMs do in software engineering.
This survey tells them \emph{how} LLMs see the repository when they do it.
Both perspectives are necessary to design the next generation of
repository-aware LLM-based SE tools.

\subsection{Research Agenda}
\label{subsec:agenda}

Based on the 11 open challenges identified in Section~\ref{subsec:challenges},
we prioritise the following three research directions for immediate attention:

\paragraph{Priority~1: Temporal freshness.}
Closing the 10--61\% gap between static-snapshot evaluation and real-world
deployment requires both new benchmark designs (with temporal controls) and
new representation techniques that support incremental, low-latency updates
for PAR-1, PAR-3, and PAR-6 systems.

\paragraph{Priority~2: Token-budget-aware paradigm hybridisation.}
The complementary strengths of PAR-2 (scalability), PAR-3 (precision),
PAR-5 (memory), and PAR-7 (flexibility) suggest that hybrid systems will
dominate the next generation of repository-level LLM tools.
A formal framework for composing paradigms within a token budget $\tau$ is the
most pressing theoretical contribution the community can make.

\paragraph{Priority~3: Evaluation standardisation.}
The field currently lacks a shared evaluation framework.
We call for a community-driven effort to establish standard repository
collections, temporal snapshots, and evaluation metrics analogous to
SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} but designed to test all seven
paradigms fairly and to control for temporal bias.
