\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern software systems are organised as large, multi-file repositories
whose artefacts—source files, dependency graphs, issue trackers, test
suites, and documentation—collectively define what a system does and how
it is structured.  When a developer needs to complete a function, fix a
bug, or translate a module, their cognitive process implicitly spans
dozens of related files and implicit structural constraints.  Enabling
Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform the same reasoning at the
repository scale is now recognised as one of the central unsolved problems
in automated software engineering
\cite{hou2024llmse_slr,husein2025llmcompletion_slr,jiang2026surveycodegen}.

The challenge is both representational and computational.  A modern
codebase may contain hundreds of thousands of tokens; no current LLM
context window can ingest this in full.  Researchers and practitioners
have consequently developed a rich ecosystem of strategies to
\textit{select}, \textit{compress}, and \textit{structure} the most
relevant fragments of a repository before presenting them to an LLM.
These strategies range from dense embedding retrieval
\cite{feng2020codebert,guo2021graphcodebert} through graph-based
structural representation
\cite{liu2024graphcoder,ouyang2025repograph} to fully agentic
exploration loops
\cite{yang2024sweagent,bairi2024codeplan}.  Yet, despite this
proliferation of techniques, no prior work has systematically mapped the
design space across the full breadth of SE objectives, established a
rigorous taxonomy, or quantitatively compared the empirical evidence
across standardised benchmarks.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A seven-paradigm taxonomy} of repository representation
    strategies (PAR-1 to PAR-7), derived from a principled thematic
    synthesis of 111 peer-reviewed studies.

  \item \textbf{A comprehensive mapping} of each paradigm to seven SE
    objectives (code completion, generation, search, bug fixing,
    refactoring, test generation, code review), quantifying coverage
    and identifying blind spots.

  \item \textbf{An evidence-based comparison} of paradigm effectiveness
    across 18 standardised benchmarks
    (e.g., SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench},
    CrossCodeEval~\cite{ding2023crosscodeeval},
    RepoBench~\cite{liu2024repobench}),
    including meta-analysis of reported metrics.

  \item \textbf{A structured trade-off analysis} across four dimensions:
    accuracy, latency, token budget, and scalability, with guidelines
    for practitioners.

  \item \textbf{An open research agenda} identifying the most
    significant unsolved problems and directions for future work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope and Exclusions}

This review focuses exclusively on repository-\textit{level}
representations: approaches that go beyond a single function or class and
explicitly exploit cross-file, project-wide, or multi-artefact context.
Purely function-level models, novel LLM architectures without a
repository-context mechanism, and non-empirical position papers are
excluded (see Section~\ref{sec:selection} for formal criteria).
