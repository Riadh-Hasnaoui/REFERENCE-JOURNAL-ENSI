\section{Gap Analysis and Research Agenda}
\label{sec:gaps}

The synthesis of 163~primary studies reveals five significant research gaps
that should shape the field's agenda over the next five years.

\paragraph{Gap 1: Multi-paradigm Evaluation Absence.}
No existing benchmark compares all seven PAR paradigms under identical
experimental conditions.  A unified evaluation harness (building on
SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} and RepoBench~\cite{liu2024repobench})
would enable apples-to-apples comparison.

\paragraph{Gap 2: Stale-Context Bias in Benchmarks.}
HumanEvo~\cite{zheng2025humanevo} demonstrates that evaluating on repository
snapshots rather than live heads biases accuracy estimates by 10–61\%.
Future benchmarks must track repository evolution to provide
ecologically valid measurements.

\paragraph{Gap 3: Underexplored Dynamic Representation (PAR-4).}
Only 8\% of the corpus exploits runtime signals (test outcomes, coverage).
There is a major opportunity to integrate execution feedback into
retrieval-augmented pipelines.

\paragraph{Gap 4: Test-Generation Coverage.}
Test generation is addressed by fewer than 15\% of PAR-3 and PAR-5 studies,
despite its industrial importance.  Coupling graph-structural context with
mutation-testing feedback is largely unexplored.

\paragraph{Gap 5: Cross-Language Generalisation.}
Over 70\% of primary studies focus exclusively on Python or Java.
The behaviour of all seven paradigms on polyglot repositories
(e.g., C++/Rust/Go interoperability) remains an open empirical question.
