\section{Research Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

This section describes the five-pillar methodology followed to conduct the
Systematic Literature Review (SLR).  The protocol was designed to satisfy the
rigorous reporting criteria of \emph{ACM Transactions on Software Engineering
and Methodology} (TOSEM) and is grounded in well-established guidance from the
software-engineering research community~\cite{kitchenham2007guidelines,
wohlin2014snowballing, page2021prisma, cruzes2011thematic,
acm_empirical_standards}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 1 — Formal Protocol (Kitchenham)}
\label{subsec:kitchenham}

The SLR follows the three-phase protocol of Kitchenham and
Charters~\cite{kitchenham2007guidelines}: (i)~\emph{Planning}—defining
research questions, search strings, and inclusion/exclusion criteria; (ii)
\emph{Conducting}—executing the search, screening, quality assessment, and
data extraction; (iii)~\emph{Reporting}—synthesising and presenting the
results.  The protocol was registered before data collection.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 2 — Database Search}
\label{subsec:search}

The primary search was executed against five bibliographic databases: ACM
Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Springer\-Link, Scopus, and arXiv/Semantic
Scholar.  The search string combined terms from three conceptual clusters:
\emph{repository representation} (\texttt{repository}, \texttt{codebase},
\texttt{project-level}), \emph{LLM-based agents} (\texttt{LLM},
\texttt{large language model}, \texttt{agent}), and \emph{software
engineering tasks} (\texttt{code completion}, \texttt{bug fixing},
\texttt{refactoring}, \texttt{test generation}).  The search covered the
period 2020–2025, yielding \textbf{892~candidate papers} after de-duplication.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 3 — Snowballing (Wohlin 2014)}
\label{subsec:snowballing}

To complement the database search and reduce publication-bias, we applied
the bidirectional snowballing procedure described by
Wohlin~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing}: one round of \emph{backward} snowballing
(tracing references of included papers) and one round of \emph{forward}
snowballing (tracing citations of included papers via Semantic Scholar).
Snowballing added \textbf{27~additional studies} (17\% of the final corpus).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Replication package.}
The complete snowballing log (all articles screened during each backward and
forward pass, with accept/reject decisions and rationale), together with the
data extraction forms and synthesis spreadsheets, are available in our online
replication package archived on Zenodo
(DOI to be assigned upon acceptance).
This is consistent with ACM TOSEM replication guidelines; only the citation
for the methodological foundation---Wohlin (2014)---is needed in the body of
the paper.
\end{quote}

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 4 — Inclusion / Exclusion Criteria}
\label{subsec:criteria}

\paragraph{Inclusion criteria (IC).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item IC1: The paper proposes, evaluates, or analyses a technique for
        representing a code repository (or a cross-file context) as input to
        an LLM or LLM-based agent.
  \item IC2: The primary SE task addressed is one of: code completion, bug
        fixing / repair, test generation, code search, or code refactoring.
  \item IC3: Published in a peer-reviewed venue or as an arXiv preprint with
        a verifiable institutional affiliation (2020–2025).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Exclusion criteria (EC).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item EC1: Papers focused solely on single-function or single-file
        completion without any cross-file or repository-level context.
  \item EC2: Purely theoretical or position papers without empirical
        evaluation.
  \item EC3: Duplicate entries, extended versions already included, or
        papers not in English.
\end{itemize}

After applying the criteria to all 892~candidates (title/abstract screening
by two reviewers; full-text screening for borderline cases), the final corpus
comprised \textbf{163~primary studies}.  Inter-rater agreement reached
$\kappa = 0.81$ (substantial agreement), with conflicts resolved by
discussion.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 5 — Quality Assessment}
\label{subsec:quality}

Each included study was scored against a five-point quality checklist adapted
from the ACM Empirical Standards~\cite{acm_empirical_standards}: (Q1)~clear
research objectives; (Q2)~reproducible experimental setup; (Q3)~benchmarked
against at least one baseline; (Q4)~threats to validity discussed;
(Q5)~publicly available artefacts (code or data).  Only studies scoring
$\geq 3/5$ were retained for the in-depth synthesis.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 6 — Data Extraction}
\label{subsec:extraction}

Data were extracted along ten analytical dimensions (see
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}): representation paradigm (PAR-1 to PAR-7), SE
task addressed, evaluation benchmark, primary metric, model backbone, token
budget, latency profile, update strategy, scalability evidence, and training
dependency.  All extraction forms are part of the replication package
(see Section~\ref{subsec:snowballing}).

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pillar 7 — Thematic Synthesis (Cruzes \& Dyb\aa)}
\label{subsec:synthesis}

Findings were synthesised using the recommended thematic synthesis steps of
Cruzes and Dyb{\aa}~\cite{cruzes2011thematic}: open coding of each primary
study, affinity grouping of codes into themes, and iterative refinement until
theoretical saturation.  The resulting taxonomy of seven \emph{Paradigms of
Repository Representation} (PAR-1 to PAR-7) is presented in
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}.
