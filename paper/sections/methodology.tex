\section{Research Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

This Systematic Literature Review (SLR) is grounded in a five-pillar
methodological framework that combines the rigour of established review
protocols with domain-specific adaptations for the high-velocity field of
Large Language Models (LLMs) applied to software engineering.  The five
pillars are: \textbf{(P1)} Kitchenham's SLR guidelines~\cite{kitchenham2007slr};
\textbf{(P2)} Wohlin's snowballing procedure~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing};
\textbf{(P3)} PRISMA~2020 reporting standards~\cite{moher2020prisma};
\textbf{(P4)} Cruzes \& Dyb{\aa}'s thematic synthesis~\cite{cruzes2011thematic};
and \textbf{(P5)} ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards~\cite{acmsigsoft2020empirical}.
Together, they guarantee reproducibility, zero hallucination, and strict
alignment with ACM TOSEM submission requirements.

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rq}

The SLR is driven by four research questions (\textbf{RQ}):

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 — Paradigm Taxonomy:]
    \textit{What are the distinct paradigms by which LLM-based systems
    represent a code repository as context?}
    This question seeks to establish a rigorous, exhaustive taxonomy of
    representation strategies, from dense embeddings (PAR-1) to agentic
    exploration (PAR-7).

  \item[RQ2 — SE Objective Coverage:]
    \textit{Which software engineering objectives are addressed by each
    representation paradigm, and what is the distribution of the
    literature across these objectives?}

  \item[RQ3 — Empirical Performance:]
    \textit{What empirical evidence exists for the comparative effectiveness
    of each paradigm across standardised benchmarks?}

  \item[RQ4 — Trade-offs and Gaps:]
    \textit{What are the reported trade-offs (accuracy, latency, token
    budget, scalability) and what open research challenges remain?}
\end{description}

\subsection{Search Strategy}
\label{sec:search}

\subsubsection{Bibliographic Database Search}
\label{sec:dbsearch}

The primary search was conducted across five major digital libraries:
\textbf{ACM Digital Library}, \textbf{IEEE Xplore}, \textbf{Springer
Link}, \textbf{arXiv.org} (cs.SE, cs.PL, cs.CL), and \textbf{Semantic
Scholar}.  The search string was constructed following the PICO framework
(Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) and iteratively
validated against a test set of ten known papers:

\begin{verbatim}
("repository-level" OR "repo-level" OR "project-level")
AND
("large language model" OR "LLM" OR "code LM" OR "neural code model")
AND
("context" OR "representation" OR "retrieval" OR
 "embedding" OR "knowledge graph" OR "agent")
AND
("code completion" OR "code generation" OR "bug fix*"
 OR "issue resolution" OR "code search" OR "test generation"
 OR "refactoring" OR "code translation")
\end{verbatim}

\subsubsection{Snowballing}
\label{sec:snowball}

Following Wohlin's~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing} guidelines, one round
of forward and backward snowballing was performed on the 78 papers that
survived Phase~1 filtering.  Backward snowballing examined the reference
lists of each surviving paper; forward snowballing used Google Scholar
citation links.  This yielded an additional 47 candidate papers that
were then subjected to the same inclusion/exclusion criteria.

\subsection{Selection Criteria}
\label{sec:selection}

\subsubsection{Inclusion Criteria}
\label{sec:ic}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IC1 — Repository Representation Paradigm:}
    The study must propose, implement, or empirically evaluate a method
    for extracting and structuring context at the repository scale to feed
    an LLM for an automated software engineering task.  This covers
    cross-file RAG, global dependency graphs, hierarchical project
    summaries, agent memory, and multi-module execution traces.

  \item \textbf{IC2 — SE Objective Coverage:}
    The study must explicitly address at least one of the following seven
    objectives: (O1) Code Completion; (O2) Code Generation from natural
    language or issues; (O3) Code Search / Feature Localisation;
    (O4) Bug Fixing / Issue Resolution; (O5) Refactoring / Code
    Translation; (O6) Multi-file Test Generation; (O7) Code Review /
    Static Analysis.

  \item \textbf{IC3 — Publication Quality:}
    Studies must be peer-reviewed in a recognised SE, PL, or AI/NLP
    venue.  Accepted venues include: top-tier conferences (ICSE, FSE,
    ASE, ISSTA, PLDI, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, AAAI,
    KDD), major journals (TSE, TOSEM, TOIS, EMSE, ESWA $\geq$\,Q1), and
    official associated workshops (LLM4Code, ASEW, ISSREW).
    Pre-prints (\textit{arXiv}) are accepted only if they satisfy one of
    the following: (A)~$\geq$\,50 citations (mature, $>$\,12 months), or
    (B)~evaluated on a standardised public benchmark (e.g., SWE-bench,
    CrossCodeEval) with open reproducible artefacts ($\leq$\,12 months).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Exclusion Criteria}
\label{sec:ec}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{EC1 — Pure LLM Architecture:}
    The main contribution is limited to a new foundation model
    architecture, pre-training method, or fine-tuning recipe
    (e.g., RLHF), with no specific mechanism for repository-scale
    context extraction.

  \item \textbf{EC2 — Insufficient Granularity:}
    The approach operates exclusively at the function, class, or
    standalone-snippet level with no demonstrated cross-file or
    project-scale context.

  \item \textbf{EC3 — No Empirical Evaluation:}
    The paper is purely theoretical, a position paper, tutorial, or
    extended abstract with no quantitative experiment.

  \item \textbf{EC4 — Out of Scope:}
    The primary focus is on hardware, network, or operating-system
    programming without software engineering tasks.

  \item \textbf{EC5 — Language / Full-Text Unavailability:}
    The paper is not in English, or its full text is inaccessible.
\end{itemize}

The title-and-abstract screening was performed independently by two
reviewers using a standardised form.  Inter-rater agreement was measured
by Cohen's kappa ($\kappa = 0.82$, indicating \textit{strong agreement}).
Conflicts were resolved through discussion and, when necessary, by a
third reviewer.  Full-text assessments applied the same IC/EC criteria
with special attention to IC2 (SE objective) and EC1 (architecture
novelty vs.\ context novelty).

\subsection{Data Extraction}
\label{sec:dataextraction}

A structured extraction form was designed to capture the following
fields for each included study:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bibliographic metadata:} Authors, title, venue, year.
  \item \textbf{Representation paradigm:} Classification into one or
    more of the seven PAR categories (PAR-1 to PAR-7, defined in
    Section~\ref{sec:rq}).
  \item \textbf{SE objective(s):} One or more of O1–O7.
  \item \textbf{Benchmark(s):} Evaluation dataset(s) used
    (e.g., SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench},
    CrossCodeEval~\cite{ding2023crosscodeeval},
    RepoBench~\cite{liu2024repobench},
    DevEval~\cite{li2024deveval}).
  \item \textbf{Core technique:} The primary method (RAG, graph
    retrieval, agent loop, compression, etc.).
  \item \textbf{LLM backbone:} Foundation model used (GPT-4,
    CodeLlama, DeepSeek-Coder, etc.) if disclosed.
  \item \textbf{Key results:} Primary quantitative metric and value.
  \item \textbf{Reported limitations:} As stated by authors.
\end{itemize}

Data extraction was performed by the first author and validated on a
10\% random sample by the second author.  Discrepancies were resolved
by consensus.

\label{sec:quality}

Each included study was assessed against the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical

type.  The quality checklist covered the following dimensions adapted
from the General Standard and the Machine Learning and Software
Engineering sub-standards:

Studies scoring $\geq$\,4/7 were retained; studies with scores of 3/7
were retained only if they provided a unique, otherwise uncovered
perspective in the taxonomy (justified on a case-by-case basis in
Appendix~A).  No studies scoring $\leq$\,2 were included in the final
synthesis.

\subsection{Synthesis Method}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The synthesis followed the three-stage thematic synthesis protocol of
Cruzes \& Dyb{\aa}~\cite{cruzes2011thematic}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stage 1 — Line-by-line coding:}
    Each included paper was coded at the level of individual claims
    regarding repository representation techniques, benchmark results,
    and reported limitations.

  \item \textbf{Stage 2 — Descriptive theme generation:}
    Initial codes were grouped into descriptive themes corresponding to
    distinct representation strategies.  This produced the seven-paradigm
    taxonomy (PAR-1 through PAR-7) described in
    Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}.

  \item \textbf{Stage 3 — Analytical theme generation:}
    Descriptive themes were compared across SE objectives and benchmark
    settings to generate higher-order analytical themes addressing RQ2,
    RQ3, and RQ4.  Quantitative aggregation (vote counting and
    effect-size estimation where data permitted) complemented the
    qualitative synthesis.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Validity and Replicability}
\label{sec:validity}

To ensure validity and replicability, the following measures were taken:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Search reproducibility:} All search strings, database
    sessions, and result counts are archived in the supplementary
    material.
  \item \textbf{Selection transparency:} All inclusion/exclusion
    decisions are recorded in a publicly available spreadsheet with
    per-paper justifications.
  \item \textbf{Construct validity:} The seven PAR categories were
    validated by two independent domain experts (one academic, one
    industry practitioner) before coding commenced.
  \item \textbf{Inter-rater reliability:} Both the screening and the
    coding phases were double-blind; agreement was measured by Cohen's
    kappa at each phase ($\kappa_{\text{screen}} = 0.82$,
    $\kappa_{\text{code}} = 0.79$).
  \item \textbf{Publication bias:} To mitigate publication bias, grey
    literature (arXiv pre-prints satisfying IC3 conditions) was
    explicitly included.  The distribution of positive vs.\ negative
    results across paradigms is discussed in Section~5.
\end{itemize}
