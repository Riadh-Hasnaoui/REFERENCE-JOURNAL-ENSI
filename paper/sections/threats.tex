\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

We evaluate threats using Wohlin et al.'s four-category framework for
empirical studies in software engineering, as adapted for systematic reviews
by Kitchenham~\cite{kitchenham2007guidelines} and mandated by the ACM SIGSOFT
Empirical Standards~\cite{acmsigsoft2020standards}.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Construct Validity}
\label{subsec:threat-construct}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Taxonomy coding subjectivity.}
The assignment of each of the 159 primary studies to one of the seven paradigms
(PAR-1 through PAR-7) relies on analyst interpretation of each paper's context
construction pipeline.
Disagreements between coders could distort paradigm population counts and
the downstream 7$\times$7 mapping matrix.

\emph{Mitigation.}
We applied a two-phase coding protocol: an independent annotation pass by each
of two coders followed by a reconciliation session for all disagreements.
Inter-Rater Reliability (IRR) was measured using Cohen's $\kappa$; the final
$\kappa = 0.81$ exceeds the ``substantial agreement'' threshold of~0.80 and
satisfies the minimum standard recommended for SLR taxonomy studies
\cite{cruzes2011recommended}.
The complete coding schema and per-study assignments are provided in
Online Supplement~B.

\paragraph{Operationalisation of the Repository Representation Problem.}
Our formal definition of the RRP as a constrained optimisation over
$\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{R}, q, \tau)$ (Section~\ref{subsec:rrp}) is one
possible formalisation.
Alternative definitions — focusing on, e.g., architectural layers rather than
context construction — could produce different taxonomy boundaries.

\emph{Mitigation.}
The RRP definition was grounded iteratively in the corpus: we used Cruzes and
Dyb{\aa}'s thematic synthesis procedure~\cite{cruzes2011recommended} to move
from the raw codes (per-paper context strategy) to higher-level categories
(paradigms), ensuring that the taxonomy reflects observable patterns in the
literature rather than a priori theoretical commitments.
All coding rounds are documented in the PRISMA 2020 checklist accompanying
this paper~\cite{page2021prisma}.

\paragraph{Measurement of the evaluation bias.}
The 10--61\% static-repository evaluation bias is quantified on the basis of a
\emph{single} benchmark, HumanEvo~\cite{zheng2025humanevo}.
It is possible that this estimate is specific to HumanEvo's repository
selection (Python projects, GitHub repositories active during 2020--2022) and
would not generalise to other languages or repository types.

\emph{Mitigation.}
We report the HumanEvo estimate with explicit labelling as a point estimate
from a single source and do not claim that 10--61\% is a universal constant.
We identify the replication of this measurement across languages, paradigms,
and repository sizes as a priority research gap
(Section~\ref{subsec:challenges}).

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Internal Validity}
\label{subsec:threat-internal}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Search completeness.}
A systematic review is only as valid as its search procedure.
Studies missed at the identification stage cannot be recovered.
The four primary databases used (IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library,
Springer Link, arXiv) cover the principal venues for SE and AI research
but may miss relevant workshop papers, grey literature, and
preprints archived in non-standard repositories.

\emph{Mitigation.}
We applied Wohlin's two-stage snowballing procedure~\cite{wohlin2014snowballing}
starting from 27 seed papers, performing one full round of forward and
backward snowballing.
Snowballing added 34 studies (21\% of the final corpus) that were not
retrieved by the keyword-based database search, confirming that snowballing
substantially increases recall for this literature.
The PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:prisma}) documents every
identification and exclusion decision.

\paragraph{Publication bias.}
Studies that adopt novel or high-performing repository representation
strategies are more likely to be published than studies with null or
negative results.
This may overestimate the observed performance of all seven paradigms and
undercount their failure modes.

\emph{Mitigation.}
Our inclusion criteria IC1--IC5 do not filter by outcome; we explicitly
included benchmark and evaluation studies regardless of whether the evaluated
system outperforms a baseline.
Studies reporting ablation or degradation results (e.g., studies showing when
RAG degrades completion quality~\cite{yang2025empirical}) are included and
counted in the corpus.
We acknowledge, however, that unpublished negative results remain invisible
to any SLR by design.

\paragraph{Temporal confounding.}
The 2020--2026 corpus spans a period of dramatic, non-linear progress in LLM
capabilities.
A technique evaluated against GPT-3.5 in 2023 is not directly comparable to
one evaluated against GPT-4o or Claude-3 Opus in 2025.
Model capability improvements partially confound paradigm performance trends.

\emph{Mitigation.}
We report paradigm dominance trends as \emph{longitudinal patterns}
(Section~\ref{subsec:trends}) and explicitly warn against cross-year
performance comparisons.
The 7$\times$7 mapping matrix (Table~\ref{tab:paradigm-task}) records
\emph{study coverage}, not performance metrics, making it immune to temporal
model-capability confounds.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{External Validity}
\label{subsec:threat-external}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Rapid obsolescence of LLM-based systems.}
The field represented by this corpus is among the fastest-moving in all of
computer science.
In the 12 months between early 2025 and early 2026 alone, SWE-bench
resolution rates improved from approximately 15\% to over 50\% for leading
agentic systems~\cite{zhang2025swebenchlive}.
There is a non-trivial risk that some findings — particularly the relative
dominance of PAR-7 (agentic) and the paradigm population counts — will shift
within months of publication.

\emph{Mitigation.}
The taxonomy itself is designed to be stable: the seven paradigms characterise
\emph{architectural families} of context construction strategies, not
individual systems.
A new system may achieve superior benchmark performance, but it will still
fall within one (or a composition of) the seven paradigms.
We release the full search-to-synthesis protocol (PRISMA 2020 flow diagram,
inclusion criteria, coding schema) so that the community can execute
incremental updates without repeating the full review.

\paragraph{Generalisability of the benchmark evidence.}
The primary evidence base for paradigm performance comparisons is dominated by
a small number of heavily used benchmarks: SWE-bench
\cite{jimenez2024swebench}, RepoBench~\cite{liu2024repobench}, and
CrossCodeEval~\cite{ding2023crosscodeeval}.
These benchmarks are primarily constructed from Python and Java repositories,
are hosted on GitHub, and reflect the repository complexity of open-source
mid-size projects.
Industrial repositories — proprietary, polyglot, orders of magnitude larger
— are substantially underrepresented.

\emph{Mitigation.}
We explicitly flag this as Open Challenge~4 (multilingual repositories) and
Open Challenge~5 (very large repositories) in Section~\ref{subsec:challenges}.
We do not extrapolate our findings to industrial settings and encourage
practitioners to treat the taxonomy as a \emph{framework for analysis}
rather than a prescriptive deployment guide.

\paragraph{Scope boundary with closely related paradigms.}
Our inclusion criterion IC2 requires that a study address at least one
cross-file dependency (i.e., that it operates at the repository level rather
than the single-file level).
This boundary is sharp in definition but fuzzy in practice: some systems
operate at the level of large, multi-section single files that effectively
function as mini-repositories.
Excluding such studies may undercount PAR-1 and PAR-6 slightly.

\emph{Mitigation.}
All borderline inclusion/exclusion decisions were adjudicated by the two
coders jointly.
The inter-rater agreement score of $\kappa = 0.81$ indicates that the IC2
boundary was applied consistently.
Excluded borderline papers are documented in Online Supplement~A.
