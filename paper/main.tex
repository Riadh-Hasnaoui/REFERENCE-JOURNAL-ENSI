\begin{abstract}
The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into software engineering
workflows has introduced a fundamental challenge: how to represent an entire
code repository---containing thousands of files, complex dependency graphs, and
rich semantic relationships---such that an LLM can effectively reason over it.
We present the first systematic literature review (SLR) that comprehensively
maps and characterizes repository-level representation approaches for LLMs.
Following a rigorous five-pillar methodology (Kitchenham \& Charters, 2007;
Wohlin, 2014; PRISMA~2020; Cruzes \& Dyb\r{a}, 2011; ACM SIGSOFT Empirical
Standards), we synthesized 262 primary studies (159 core + 103 benchmarks)
published between 2019 and 2025. Our analysis yields four contributions:
(C1)~a multi-dimensional taxonomy of seven representation paradigms across ten
structural properties; (C2)~a bidirectional Representation$\times$Objective
mapping matrix ($7\times7$); (C3)~a formal trade-off framework across five
operational dimensions; and (C4)~a systematic gap analysis of 25 benchmarks
and a concrete research agenda. Our results reveal that no single
representation paradigm is universally optimal, and that the static-repository
assumption introduces a systematic evaluation bias of 10\%--61\%
(HumanEvo~\cite{zheng2025humanevo}).
\end{abstract}
