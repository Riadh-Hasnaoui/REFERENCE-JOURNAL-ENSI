\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models~(LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in a range
of software engineering tasks—code completion, generation, bug fixing, and code
search—yet their deployment on real-world software repositories remains fundamentally
challenging. A \emph{code repository} is not a single file: it is a complex, evolving
artifact comprising thousands of interdependent files, in-house APIs, build systems, and
test suites. How an LLM-based system extracts, structures, and delivers repository-scale
context to the model is the key engineering decision that determines the ceiling of its
downstream performance.

This paper presents the first \textbf{systematic literature review~(SLR) and taxonomy}
of code repository representation approaches for LLM-based software engineering,
synthesising 168 primary studies published between 2020 and early 2026.  We follow a
PRISMA-2020-compliant protocol with three inclusion criteria, six exclusion criteria,
forward/backward snowballing, and thematic synthesis.  From this corpus we derive a
\textbf{seven-dimensional taxonomy} classifying approaches by representational
mechanism: \emph{(PAR-1)} dense embedding and prompt construction,
\emph{(PAR-2)} retrieval-augmented generation~(RAG),
\emph{(PAR-3)} graph-based and structural representation,
\emph{(PAR-4)} execution-based and dynamic representation,
\emph{(PAR-5)} memory-augmented context,
\emph{(PAR-6)} compression and summarization, and
\emph{(PAR-7)} agentic and iterative context exploration.
For each paradigm we report the representational mechanism, the downstream SE tasks
addressed, the evaluation benchmarks used, and key empirical findings.  We further
survey 25 evaluation benchmarks, analysing their scope, language coverage, and
alignment with practical SE objectives.  Our analysis reveals critical open challenges:
the lack of unified cross-paradigm benchmarks, the scalability–precision trade-off in
graph-based approaches, and the context-window bottleneck for million-line repositories.
We conclude with a research agenda to guide future work.
\end{abstract}
