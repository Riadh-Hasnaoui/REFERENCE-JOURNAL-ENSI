\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly employed as the backbone
of automated software engineering (SE) tools that operate at the
repository level—spanning code completion, bug fixing, refactoring, and
test generation.  However, no single LLM context window can accommodate
a modern codebase in its entirety.  Consequently, a rich ecosystem of
\emph{repository representation} strategies has emerged to select,
compress, and structure relevant code fragments before presenting them
to a model.  This paper presents a Systematic Literature Review (SLR)
of 111 peer-reviewed studies published between 2020 and early 2026.
Grounded in Kitchenham's guidelines, Wohlin's snowballing procedure,
PRISMA~2020 reporting standards, Cruzes \& Dyb{\aa}'s thematic
synthesis, and the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards, the review
establishes a seven-paradigm taxonomy (\emph{PAR-1: Dense Embedding},
\emph{PAR-2: RAG Cross-File}, \emph{PAR-3: Graph-Based},
\emph{PAR-4: Dynamic/Execution}, \emph{PAR-5: Memory-Augmented},
\emph{PAR-6: Compression/Summarisation}, and \emph{PAR-7: Agentic
Exploration}).  We map each paradigm to seven SE objectives, compare
empirical performance across 18 standardised benchmarks, and identify
the key trade-offs along four dimensions: accuracy, latency, token
budget, and scalability.  The review concludes with an open research
agenda highlighting the most critical unsolved problems.
\end{abstract}
